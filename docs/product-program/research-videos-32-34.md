## 32. I Redesigned the ENTIRE YouTube UI from Scratch

[Juxtopposed, 19:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OUM6XmhViN4).

This video exposes a manual English caption track. The two interviews below use
automatic English captions.

At 00:58–01:37 the designer addresses inconsistent icons and page styles. At
06:33–06:50 she treats Watch Later as a direct action. At 09:52–10:24 she argues
against making an exit harder to reach. At 17:05–17:53 she separates search
results from recommendations and makes filters accessible.

Apply to C: consistent native controls, direct save, an accessible focus exit,
and relevant search results. Inspect the current implementation before changing
any of these. Reject copying vote counts, social feeds, autoplay or public stats.
This is a designer's speculative redesign informed by viewer comments, with no
usability trial or measured retention result. Its claims about YouTube's motives
are interpretations. Frames at 06:35, 09:45, 17:10 and 18:50 show the discussed
interfaces and final design. Follow-up frames inspect the action/filter changes.

## 33. Thomas Petit: You're Paying Meta to Find Users Who Will Never Convert

[Paddle interview, 25:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mR9d0EEodEI).

At 02:15–02:57 Petit considers the whole path from acquisition through activation.
At 11:15–13:33 he distinguishes an optimization signal from the outcome a business
actually values. At 14:33–14:55 he says his earlier guidance caused premature
engineering in early-stage teams. At 16:07–17:19 he warns that testing has data
and opportunity costs. At 18:46–21:48 he advises small teams to focus one path
before adding payment-channel complexity.

Apply to C/D: keep the reading promise coherent and prioritize visible defects.
Apply to later A7: distinguish a tap or trial from useful reading, return and
paid value. Reject adding ad SDKs, predictive profiling, a web checkout or a
second backend during this program. This is practitioner judgment and anecdotes,
not a disclosed controlled experiment. Frames at 02:10, 12:05, 17:20 and 21:00
show a seated interview, not campaign dashboards or an independently verified
result. Do not infer a Hadithly outcome from the headline.

## 34. S07, E30: MDM Mailbag #8 with Thomas Petit

[Mobile Dev Memo, 54:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iD_YbEW0Y3w).

At 02:36–04:16 the speakers distinguish attributed conversions from incremental
effects. At 17:48–19:22 Petit separates the platform's proxy from the business
outcome. At 24:23–26:47 he recommends finding a credible message before paid
acquisition. At 35:47–40:29 he rejects a universal best onboarding length and
considers the whole flow. At 41:24–42:38 the discussion considers refunds and
readers who decline payment alongside short-term conversion.

Apply to D: show a clear, truthful reading benefit. Apply to C: retain a visible
paywall exit and useful free reading. Reject deceptive urgency, hidden prices,
difficult cancellation, extensive profiling and a new web funnel. The owner's
monthly-equivalent prototype remains separate from annual-total release approval.
This sponsored practitioner discussion supplies no controlled Hadithly evidence.
Its vendor, legal and fee commentary was not independently verified and supplies
no current policy guidance. Frames at 03:20, 18:30, 36:45 and 49:10 inspect the
discussion format; they do not reveal underlying experiment data.
